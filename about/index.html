About

I'm an engineering manager with about ten years in the industry, most of it as a full-stack engineer working across React, TypeScript, and GraphQL, before moving into management. I led the checkout experiences org at PayPal, and before that worked in engineering at Keller Williams during its shift toward becoming a technology company rather than a real estate franchise business.

How I work

I believe most team problems are actually structure problems. Give people clarity, a path to grow, and fast honest feedback, and the rest tends to follow. A few things that are true about how I lead:

  • I give feedback in real time, not just at review cycles. Specific, tied to concrete examples, and delivered as close to the moment as possible. Waiting six months to tell someone what's working, or not, helps no one.
  • I build structure before I need it. Onboarding plans, dashboards, documented processes. I'd rather invest early than rely on tribal knowledge that walks out the door with one departure.
  • I invest deeply in people who want to grow, especially the quiet ones. Some of my proudest wins as a manager have been helping someone go from silent in meetings to leading a project, or building the case that got a junior engineer promoted after leadership initially said no.
  • I diagnose root cause on underperformance, fast. Is it a fixable skill gap or a fundamental mismatch? I'd rather know early and act with clarity than let ambiguity drag out for either of us.
  • I go find the blockers outside my own org, because that's usually where the real ones live. Some of my highest-leverage work has come from relationships and trust built with teams I didn't manage.

What that actually looks like

  • Give me feedback directly, in the moment, even if it's blunt. I'd rather hear it right away than have it softened or delayed. That said, I know this style isn't for everyone, so the first time we work together, I ask how you like to receive feedback. No judgment either way, I just want it to actually be useful for you.
  • 1:1s run on a running doc, not status updates. Write things down as they come up during the week, and I'll respond or gather information before we even sit down. Every 3-4 sessions, one is dedicated entirely to career, not day-to-day work.
  • We always start with how things are outside of work. Family, pets, weekend plans, whatever's actually going on. Work comes second in that conversation, not first.
  • I default to async first. I like to be prepared, so give me a chance to think something through and respond thoughtfully rather than expecting an instant reaction. When something's genuinely urgent, that changes, and I'll drop everything.
  • I stay calm in the moment, consistently, according to every manager who's ever given me feedback. Working through several years of rapid change taught me that most of what feels urgent in the moment settles with a little perspective. My job is to take it all in and stay steady so the people around me can too.
  • I default to assuming good intent. Most people want to do a good job, and they're happiest when they're actually doing one. Starting from that assumption changes how I handle almost every disagreement or misstep on a team.
  • I give people the benefit of the doubt, but disrespect toward colleagues is where that stops. Most performance issues have a root cause worth understanding first, and I'll always look for it. But being rude or unprofessional to teammates isn't a skill gap I can coach around. That's the one thing I won't tolerate on a team, no matter how strong someone's individual output is.
